0:18 to 0:33. About 15 seconds. SAY: "The odds are long. UK employers report roughly 140 applications for every graduate vacancy and about 100 for every internship. Even a rough reading puts the chance of one application succeeding at around one in a hundred. And it varies hugely by sector, from 69 applications per hire in legal to 290 in retail. Where you aim changes the odds." SOURCES AND HOW EACH FIGURE WAS DERIVED. Please verify before presenting: these came from search summaries of the original reports, not from opening the reports. 1. About 140 applications per graduate vacancy: Institute of Student Employers (ISE) Student Recruitment Survey 2025 (2024–25 cycle), covering 155 member employers, over 1.8 million applications and more than 31,000 student hires. Mainly large UK employers with formal graduate programmes, so it does not represent the whole market. 2. About 100 applications per internship: ISE reported 101 per internship vacancy in 2024–25. Handshake’s Internships Index 2025 (US) reported an average of 109 applications per internship for 2024–25. 3. "1 in about 140" is an ESTIMATE made for this deck. It divides one successful application by the ISE figure of about 140 applications per vacancy and assumes about one hire per vacancy. Vacancies with several hires would make the odds somewhat better. It is an average across all applicants, not any one student’s chance. 4. 69 per hire in legal and 290 in retail, FMCG and tourism: ISE 2025 sector figures (built environment 73, health and pharmaceuticals 220). 5. At least 15 applications per offer: a survey of 317 early-career researchers applying for faculty posts, reported in Nature (2020). This is academia, not general graduate hiring. Blog estimates of applications per job offer range from about 30 to over 200, but they are not survey data and are not used. WHY IT MATTERS FOR ROLECRAFT: if each application has low odds, applying everywhere is a poor use of limited hours. The product helps a person choose where their evidence is strong and what to do first.
